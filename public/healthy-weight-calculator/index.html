Adult BMI screening reference

What weight range corresponds to your height?

Enter centimetres or feet and inches to convert the adult BMI 18.5–24.9 reference into kilograms and pounds. Use the result as a screening range, not a personal ideal-weight target.

The calculation

BMI divides weight in kilograms by height in metres squared. Rearranging that equation gives the weight associated with a selected BMI at a given height.

Lower weight = 18.5 × height²
Upper weight = 24.9 × height²

What the range means

The result is a population screening reference for adults. It can help start a conversation about health risk, but it does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, fitness or metabolic health.

Why muscular people need more context

BMI uses only height and weight. A trained lifter can have a higher BMI because of muscle mass, while another person with the same BMI may have a different waist measurement and body composition. Review weight alongside strength, waist circumference, health markers and longer-term trends.

When this calculator is not appropriate

  • People under 18 need age- and sex-specific growth references.
  • Pregnancy changes the meaning of body weight and BMI.
  • Older adults and some ethnic populations may require additional context.
  • Medical decisions should use a qualified clinician and appropriate measurements.

Use a range, not one perfect number

Weight naturally changes with hydration, food, glycogen and the time of measurement. If you are monitoring progress, compare measurements under similar conditions and focus on multi-week trends.
